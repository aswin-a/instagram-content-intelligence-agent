# How "performs better" is judged

Raw likes are misleading on Instagram: big accounts get more of everything, likes are often hidden, and Reels reach people who don't follow the account. So the agent normalises everything and compares like with like.

| Metric | Formula | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate (ER) | (likes + comments) ÷ followers | All formats, when likes are visible |
| View rate | views ÷ followers | Reels (the main signal when likes are hidden) |
| × account median | post metric ÷ that account's median for the same format | Outlier detection; fair comparison across account sizes |

## Rules
- **Compare within a format.** Reels vs Reels, carousels vs carousels.
- **Outlier = ≥ 2× the account's own median** for that format.
- **Small samples:** fewer than ~10 posts from an account in scope → its conclusions are marked *tentative*.
- **Tier-aware:** tier 3 (reach-context) accounts are never used as a metric benchmark for peers.
- **Never estimate.** Hidden or unreadable numbers are `not available`. A post with no usable metric can still be analysed qualitatively, labelled as such.
- **Intent beats vanity.** Buying-intent comments (price, delivery area, suitability) and the CTA type are recorded with every post and weighed in patterns.

## What counts as a pattern
A feature (theme, hook type, format, CTA, timing, caption style) that shows up in outperforming posts across **≥ 2 accounts or ≥ 3 posts**. Anything less is reported as a single example.
